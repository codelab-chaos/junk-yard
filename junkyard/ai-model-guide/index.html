3 Fewer tries, lower cost

  1. Draft cheap, finish expensive. Explore the composition on a fast, cheap model (Muse Image, FLUX Schnell, a Flash or Lite tier), then rerun the winning prompt once on a top model. On Leonardo, Flow State drafts at 1 token an image.
  2. Use unlimited modes for rerolls. Subscriptions often have a slow, unlimited lane: Midjourney Relax, Leonardo's relaxed mode on its own models (Premium and up), "unlimited" Nano Banana on some aggregators. Pay-per-use APIs have no such lane.
  3. Low quality before high. Most models price by quality tier or resolution. Pick the seed and composition at the lowest setting, then rerun only the keeper at full quality.
  4. Upscale only the final. Upscalers can cost more than every attempt that produced the image.
  5. Edit, don't reroll. When an image is 90% right, fix the rest with a reference-aware model (GPT Image, Nano Banana, Kontext) or an editor, and for video an editing model such as Aleph, instead of starting over.
  6. Batches cost per image. Four images per click cuts clicks per good result, not cost. Drop to one or two on expensive models.
  7. Video: shortest, lowest resolution first. Cost scales with seconds, resolution and audio. Test motion at 480p or 720p without audio, then render the final once.
  8. Compare dollars, not credits. Every app has its own credit. Convert to dollars per result; the maker's API price is the baseline an app has to beat.

4 Where to run them

Most of these models are not exclusive. The same Nano Banana, GPT Image, FLUX, Kling or Veo runs on its maker's API and on several multi-model apps. Apps add an interface, editing tools and sometimes unlimited lanes; APIs are the price baseline. Credits are not comparable between platforms; compare dollars per result.

5 How to read this guide

Cost per good result

Price per run × typical tries. By default the run is priced at the maker's API rate. Pick a Leonardo plan at the top to see the same models inside one app subscription: tokens per run × tries × the plan's dollars per token, assuming you use the whole allowance.

Where prices come from

Each price is marked M the maker's published API price, L published by Leonardo, 3 reported by a third party, or E an estimate. Leonardo tokens and API dollars convert at about 1,200 tokens per dollar, where Leonardo's published pairs land. Check the price in the app before a big batch.

Ratings and tries

Editor's ratings from maker docs, public leaderboards (Artificial Analysis, LMArena), Leonardo's model guides and hands-on reviews. The overall score weights image or video quality most, then prompt following, then the other axes, with speed counting least. Nobody publishes a measured "attempts per good result" figure, so tries are estimates for a simple brief. Text-heavy or multi-subject briefs take more.
